Comprehension

20 reading comprehension passages

Open a passage, read it once and answer the questions.

Education is the most powerful tool for personal and national growth. It teaches people to think critically, solve problems and respect others. Countries that invest in schools and universities usually enjoy stronger economies and fewer social conflicts. However, education is not only about marks. A student who learns honesty, discipline and curiosity gains far more than one who only memorises answers.

The Indus is the lifeline of Pakistan. It rises in the Tibetan plateau and flows through the whole country before meeting the Arabian Sea. Millions of farmers depend on its water for wheat, rice and cotton. In recent years, melting glaciers and unplanned water use have raised concerns about the river's future, so experts urge careful planning and conservation.

Climate change is caused mainly by the burning of fossil fuels, which releases greenhouse gases into the air. These gases trap heat and raise global temperatures. As a result, glaciers melt, sea levels rise and weather patterns become extreme. Planting trees, using clean energy and reducing waste are practical steps every citizen can take.

Time is the one resource that cannot be bought back. Successful students make a daily plan, set priorities and avoid distractions such as endless scrolling. They also rest properly because a tired mind learns slowly. A simple rule is to do the hardest task first, when energy is highest, and leave lighter tasks for later.

Online platforms have changed the way young people study. A student in a small town can now watch lectures from top teachers and practise with free quizzes. Yet technology has a cost: social media can waste hours if used carelessly. Digital learning works best when students set clear goals and limit entertainment during study time.

Honesty is a quiet strength. An honest person may lose a little in the short run, but gains trust, which lasts a lifetime. Trust opens doors that money cannot. In exams, in business and in friendship, a single lie can undo years of good reputation, while truthfulness slowly builds respect.

Muhammad Ali Jinnah, the founder of Pakistan, was a lawyer by profession and a leader of rare discipline. He believed in unity, faith and discipline, and encouraged the youth to work hard for the nation. His honesty and firm principles earned him respect even from his opponents.

Only a tiny share of the world's water is fresh and easy to use. Yet households waste large amounts through leaking taps, long showers and careless washing. Farmers can save water with drip irrigation, while cities can recycle wastewater. If everyone takes small steps, future generations will not face severe shortages.

Reading widens the mind and improves language. A person who reads daily learns new words, understands different cultures and writes more clearly. Even twenty minutes a day builds a strong habit. Books also help us relax, since a good story can quiet a busy mind better than a noisy screen.

Sleep is not wasted time. During sleep the brain sorts what we have learned and stores it in memory. Students who stay awake all night before an exam often forget more than they gain. Most teenagers need about eight hours of sleep to concentrate well and stay healthy.

Today's youth are the first generation to grow up with smartphones. They can learn coding, start online businesses and connect with people worldwide. At the same time, they face cyberbullying and constant comparison on social media. Parents and teachers should guide them to use technology wisely rather than ban it completely.

Forests are called the lungs of the earth because trees absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen. They also protect soil from erosion and give shelter to countless animals. Despite this, vast areas are cut every year for farms and cities. Replanting trees and protecting wild habitats are urgent tasks for every country.

Sport teaches more than winning. Players learn discipline, patience and respect for rules. A team game such as cricket or hockey shows that individual talent is not enough without cooperation. Even defeat has value, because it teaches humility and the determination to improve.

No great achievement is the work of one person alone. Even a brilliant scientist depends on colleagues, teachers and earlier discoveries. Teamwork combines different strengths and covers each member's weaknesses. Teams succeed when they share goals, communicate clearly and respect each other's ideas.

Space exploration has given humanity far more than photographs of distant planets. Satellites now help forecast weather, guide ships and connect phone calls across continents. Research for space missions has also led to better medical devices and lighter materials. Critics say the cost is high, but supporters argue the long-term benefits justify it.

Coal and oil will not last forever and they pollute the air. Solar, wind and hydropower are renewable because they use natural sources that are constantly replaced. Pakistan receives strong sunlight for most of the year, so solar power has great potential. The challenge is the high initial cost of panels and batteries.

English has become the main language of science, business and the internet. A student who speaks it well can read research papers, apply for international scholarships and communicate with people across the world. Learning English does not mean forgetting one's mother tongue; it means gaining one more key to opportunity.

Volunteers give their time without payment, yet they receive something valuable in return: experience, friendship and a sense of purpose. Whether cleaning a park or teaching children, volunteers learn leadership and empathy. Societies become stronger when ordinary people take responsibility for improving their own communities.

Every year millions of tons of waste end up in landfills. Much of it, such as paper, glass and metal, can be recycled into new products. Recycling saves energy, reduces pollution and protects natural resources. Separating household waste into different bins is a small habit that makes a big difference.

An entrepreneur sees a problem and builds a business to solve it. Starting a company is risky, and many first attempts fail. But failure often teaches the lessons that later success needs. Young entrepreneurs who stay patient, listen to customers and manage money carefully have the best chance to grow.

Verbal reasoning FAQs

How many verbal reasoning questions are in the USAT?

Public guides describe 20 verbal reasoning MCQs. Confirm the latest pattern with HEC.

How can I improve my vocabulary for the USAT?

Learn ten words a day with a synonym and an antonym, revise them weekly and use them in sentences.

How do I solve comprehension passages faster?

Read the questions first, then read the passage once, and find the answer in the text instead of guessing.
